Current SIP Construction Specification
PECASA’s current structural approach uses Structural Insulated Panels (SIPs) as standard. SIPs combine structural oriented strand board (OSB) faces with a rigid insulation core, creating an insulated structural envelope when designed and installed correctly. PECASA states that its panels are manufactured and pre-assembled off site at its local Watford facility, reducing cutting, waste and disruption in the customer’s garden.
SIP structural shell: walls, roof and floor are formed using the SIP system in PECASA’s published garden-room specification.
Foundation interface: the SIP structure is commonly supported on a project-specific ground-screw grid, subject to the site survey, ground conditions and structural requirements.
Weather protection: the roof is finished with a high-performance EPDM membrane, with exterior cladding and high-performance glazing completing the envelope.
Internal finish: PECASA’s current published build standard includes a Tape-and-joint internal finish, lighting, electrics and flooring.
Panel thickness is project-specific: PECASA does not publish one universal SIP thickness for every building. As a recent reference point, its July 2026 Bricket Wood project used 120 mm PIR insulation.
Why this matters: thermal comfort is determined by the complete envelope – walls, floor, roof, glazing, air-tightness, ventilation and heating/cooling choices – rather than by a single insulation number.

Planning Permission and Building Regulations
Many detached garden rooms used for purposes incidental to the main house can fall within Class E householder permitted-development rights in England, but that is not automatic. The design must satisfy the relevant limits and conditions, and permitted-development rights can be restricted or removed for a particular property.
Class E is for buildings used for purposes incidental to the enjoyment of the house. It does not cover separate self-contained accommodation or primary residential use such as a bedroom, bathroom or kitchen as the main living accommodation.
As a headline rule, buildings and other relevant additions cannot cover more than 50% of the curtilage around the original house, and height/location limits apply. A building within 2 metres of a boundary is generally limited to 2.5 metres overall height if relying on Class E permitted development.
Listed buildings, conservation/designated areas, Article 4 directions, planning conditions and previously removed permitted-development rights can change the position. A Lawful Development Certificate may be useful where certainty is important.
For London properties, the correct planning authority is the relevant London borough. Dense plots, conservation areas, listed buildings and restricted access make a site-specific check especially important. PECASA’s own projects demonstrate both outcomes: its Wimbledon and Mill Hill case studies state that planning permission was not required, while larger or more complex projects may need formal consent.
Building Regulations are separate from planning permission. Planning Portal guidance states that a detached outbuilding under 15 m² with no sleeping accommodation will not normally need Building Regulations approval. Between 15 m² and 30 m², approval will not normally be required if there is no sleeping accommodation and the building is either at least 1 metre from a boundary or is constructed substantially of non-combustible materials. Larger buildings, sleeping accommodation, drainage, certain electrical/plumbing works and other circumstances can trigger additional requirements. The final position should be checked for the individual project.

Do garden rooms need planning permission in London?
Most garden rooms fall under permitted development, so planning permission is often not required. Requirements may vary depending on size and location.
How long does it take to build a garden room?
Most garden rooms can be completed within 2–4 weeks depending on the design and size.
Can garden rooms be used all year?
Yes. Our garden rooms are fully insulated and designed for comfortable use throughout the year.
How much does a garden room cost in London?

What can a garden room be used for?
Garden rooms can be used as home offices, gyms, studios, guest rooms, or relaxation spaces.
Do garden rooms add value to a property?
Yes. A well designed garden room can increase the functionality and value of your home.
