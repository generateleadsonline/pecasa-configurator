Why Test Ground Screws?
Ground conditions change from one property to the next.
Clay, made ground, loose soil and other conditions all affect how a ground screw foundation performs. A site test provides real information from the proposed building location — rather than relying on assumptions.
Sites can contain a wide mix of ground conditions, sometimes within a few metres of each other:
Depending on the project, testing can help establish:
The load capacity of the installed ground screw
Performance under compression
Pull-out resistance where required
Installation torque
Suitability of the proposed screw
Whether the proposed foundation design needs adjustment
Testing Methods
Four ways we put a ground screw to the test.
Downward Load
Compression Load Testing
Buildings transfer significant downward forces into their foundations. We apply a controlled downward load to a test screw and record its response for the structural engineer.
Uplift Resistance
Tensile & Pull-Out Testing
Where a ground screw needs to resist uplift forces, we test how the installed screw responds to a controlled upward load.
Engineer-Specified
Proof Load Testing
Where a structural engineer has specified particular proof loads, we test in accordance with the project requirements and our equipment's capability.
During Installation
Torque Monitoring
Installation torque provides additional information about ground resistance, monitored and recorded as each screw is installed.
Professional Equipment
RADIX ground screw testing equipment.
Pecasa has invested in professional RADIX testing equipment to carry out controlled ground screw testing on site. A measured load is applied to the test screw so its behaviour can be recorded — giving contractors, structural engineers, architects and clients much better information when designing a ground screw foundation.
