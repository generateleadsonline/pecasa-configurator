Ground screws are a cost-effective and practical option for traditional concrete foundations. The effectiveness of any foundation system is always determined by site specific ground conditions and the soil reaction capacities of the installed screws. This is why the testing and assessment of a site can be an essential factor in determining whether or not a proposed ground screw foundation can fit into an assigned project.

The Importance of Ground Screw Testing
Ground conditions vary widely between properties, and even across different areas of the same site. Different soil types affects the performance and capacity of a ground screw foundation: any type of clay, loose ground, made ground and other variable conditions.
Site tests make ground truth data available as opposed to abstract assumptions or generalized soil information.
Based on the project and engineering requirements, ground screw testing could help to decide:
Load capacity of installed ground screw
Performance under compression
Pull-out resistance where required
Installation torque
The suitability check of the ground screw proposed
Whether changes to the foundation design are needed
Provided the testing results are documented, they can also offer technical information for the structural engineer in reviewing the acceptability of a proposed foundation system, including but not limited to projects requiring structural calculations or Building Control approval.
