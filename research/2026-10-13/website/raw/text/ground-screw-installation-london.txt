Ground Screw Installation London for SIP, Modular and Garden Buildings
Pecasa provides ground screw installation in London for suitable garden rooms, SIP buildings, modular structures and other lightweight projects. The service can include site assessment, test screws, project-specific testing, supply and installation, with the final foundation arrangement governed by the structural requirements and ground conditions.
Ground-screw service overview
Pecasa provides ground-screw support for surveying, testing, supplying and installing screw foundations for suitable lightweight buildings. Its service can cover site assessment, test-screw installation, controlled testing, supply, installation and coordination with structural engineers, architects, contractors and Building Control.
Pecasa’s published ground-screw service covers site surveys, test screws, compression/load tests, pull-out/tensile tests, torque monitoring, supply and installation.
What a ground screw foundation is
Ground screws are steel foundation elements installed into the ground to create support points for a structure. On suitable lightweight projects they can reduce the need for excavated concrete footings and can limit spoil, wet trades and disturbance on site.
The foundation still needs to be designed for the building loads and ground conditions. Screw type, depth, spacing, layout and connection to the structure above should follow the structural requirements rather than a one-size-fits-all rule.
Where the system can be used
Pecasa lists garden rooms, garden offices, annexes, SIP buildings, modular structures, timber-frame buildings, decking and other suitable lightweight projects among its ground-screw applications. Because Pecasa also builds SIP garden rooms, it has practical experience coordinating the support grid with a panelised structure above.
Ground screws are not automatically the right answer for every site. Obstructions, ground conditions, structural loads and access can point to a different solution, so the foundation method should be selected on evidence.
Survey and testing before full installation
Site assessment can include a test screw and controlled testing where the project requires evidence. Pecasa publishes compression/load testing, pull-out or tensile testing, proof-load testing and installation torque monitoring as available services.
If a structural engineer specifies a test load, method or acceptance criterion, that project brief should be provided before testing. The installer gathers the site evidence; the relevant designer determines how it is used within the foundation design.
Installation sequence and records
A typical project may move through site review, foundation layout, test screw where required, testing, confirmation of the screw arrangement, installation, level checks and recording of relevant installation data. The exact sequence depends on the structural and project brief.
Once an accepted screw foundation is installed, construction can often proceed without a concrete curing period. That can suit prefabricated and SIP structures where the superstructure is ready to follow quickly.
Practical considerations for London sites
Urban and residential sites can have narrow side access, steps, controlled parking, established landscaping and limited working space. The access route for installation or test equipment should therefore be reviewed before attendance, not discovered after the team arrives.
Provide photographs of gates, passages, steps and the proposed foundation area, together with drawings and any known underground-service information. Accurate site information makes the quotation and work plan more reliable.
Working with engineers and Building Control
Pecasa positions its service as technical support for the wider project team. Where a structural engineer defines a proof load, test method, reporting requirement or acceptance criterion, that brief should control the site work. The testing or installation contractor should not replace the structural designer’s responsibility where formal engineering is required.
Recorded information can help the engineer assess whether the foundation arrangement is appropriate or needs adjustment. The documentation requirements should be agreed before the visit if results will be submitted for formal review.
Ground screws versus concrete
Ground screws can offer less excavation, reduced concrete use, faster installation and lower disruption on suitable lightweight projects. They also avoid a concrete curing period before the supported structure can progress.
Those advantages do not make ground screws universally superior. The correct foundation follows the ground, structural loads, geometry, access and engineering requirements. A professional recommendation should be able to explain why the proposed system is suitable for the particular project.
What to send when requesting a quotation
Provide the site address, proposed building type, approximate dimensions, drawings where available, structural loads or engineer requirements and photographs of the full access route. If a specific test or reporting format is required, include that instruction at the start.
The foundation should follow the structural loads and site conditions; ground screws are an option for suitable projects, not a universal replacement for every foundation type.

Practical checks before you decide
For ground screw installation London, provide the project drawings, site information and any structural engineer requirements before the visit is booked.
Questions to resolve before approval
Confirm the final external and internal dimensions, finished floor level, glazing orientation, access route, service connections and any landscaping that must remain protected. If there are trees, inspection chambers, retaining walls or known buried services near the proposed work, flag them before the foundation or service design is finalised.
Ask the proposal to distinguish fixed inclusions from optional items and assumptions. A detailed scope reduces misunderstandings and makes later changes easier to price and approve.
Think about long-term use
Design decisions should reflect how the space or system may be used in several years, not only the immediate requirement. Extra service capacity, adaptable layouts, maintainable connections and clear documentation can preserve options without materially changing the first-day appearance.
Long-term value is usually driven by the fundamentals: a suitable foundation, sound structural design, weather protection, thermal performance where relevant, competent installation and straightforward maintenance. Decorative features are easier to change later.
A final point before you book
A quotation for ground screw installation should state the proposed method, equipment, scope, records and any assumptions that depend on site conditions.
Frequently Asked Questions
What buildings can use ground screws?
Pecasa lists garden rooms, offices, annexes, SIP and modular buildings, timber-frame structures, decking and other suitable lightweight projects.
Do ground screws need testing?
Requirements vary. A structural engineer may request compression, pull-out, proof-load or other evidence depending on the design and ground conditions.
Can installation reduce concrete use?
On suitable projects a ground-screw system can reduce or avoid conventional concrete footings, subject to the structural design.
Does Pecasa supply and install?
Yes. Pecasa publishes a combined service covering survey, testing, supply and professional installation, or standalone elements as required.
