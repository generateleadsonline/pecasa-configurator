Between the sites, ground conditions can be significantly different. If the screw can produce a preliminary test results at real project site, then it can shed light over the performance of tube screws on existing soil.
Controlled testing provides a solution to this by applying loads to a test screw and monitoring its response while data is recorded, rather than assuming the ground conditions it may see. This information can then be given to the project structural engineer for review if necessary.
Ground Screw Compression Testing
Ground screws are engineered to safely transmit structural loads into the earth. What is Compression Testing? Compression testing evaluates the response of a ground screw under a controlled static axial load in compression.
Pecasa also performs compression tests in areas indicated by the project engineer. Test results can yield valuable project-related information regarding the behaviour of the proposed foundation system.
This may be particularly useful in cases where the structure needs to satisfy the engineer on how much uplift force or weight the ground screw must meet at least under the design loads expected.
Tensile and Pull-Out Testing
Ground screws may also need to be added in order to resist an uplift or tensile (pull down) force
Where appropriate, Pecasa can perform tensile pull-out testing by applying an upward load to test the installed screw.
This is of specific relevance to structures exposed to wind uplift or other forces that may be acting in tension on the foundation.
The structural engineer responsible for the project and intended design conditions should always dictate testing requirements.

A More Conversational Alternative to Traditional Land acknowledgements
For the right project, ground screws are a direct alternative to concrete foundations.
They can minimize the excavation and concrete that may be needed for a particular project, as well as permitting the installation of foundations with less impact on the surrounding area.
Ground screws can allow you to build without waiting for large concrete foundations to harden, and this is what makes them appealing on many of today’s timber-frame, SIPs, garden room and modular builds.
Nevertheless, a ground screw might not be right for your job site and structural loads; subsoil conditions & engineering requirements can influence its appropriateness.
