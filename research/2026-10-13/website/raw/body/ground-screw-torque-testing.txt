What installation torque means
As a ground screw advances into the ground, the installation equipment experiences rotational resistance. Recording that resistance can provide information about changes in ground response and consistency between screw locations.
Torque is an installation parameter, not automatically a structural capacity certificate. Any relationship between torque and load capacity needs a suitable technical basis for the screw system, ground conditions and design and should be accepted by the responsible engineer.
Torque monitoring and direct load testing are different
A compression test applies a known downward load and measures response. A pull-out test assesses tensile behaviour. Torque monitoring records resistance during installation. Each method answers a different technical question.
Depending on the design, an engineer may use one form of evidence, combine several or specify a different verification method. The testing plan should therefore be agreed before site work begins.
Why traceable records matter
For a multi-screw foundation, each reading should be relatable to the correct position on the layout. Screw identification, location, installation information and anomalies should be recorded in a form the project team can understand later.
If a reading differs markedly from nearby positions, the installer should record and escalate it rather than assuming the whole site is uniform. The engineer can then decide whether further testing or a design change is necessary.

Ground screws versus concrete
Ground screws can offer less excavation, reduced concrete use, faster installation and lower disruption on suitable lightweight projects. They also avoid a concrete curing period before the supported structure can progress.
Those advantages do not make ground screws universally superior. The correct foundation follows the ground, structural loads, geometry, access and engineering requirements. A professional recommendation should be able to explain why the proposed system is suitable for the particular project.
