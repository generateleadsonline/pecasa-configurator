Garden Annexes London – Bespoke, Insulated Living Space Designed Around Your Family

A well-planned Garden Annexe can create valuable additional living accommodation within the grounds of your existing home while keeping family members close. PECASA designs, manufactures and installs bespoke annexes for homeowners who need a comfortable, private and practical space for parents, adult children, relatives, long-term guests or changing family requirements.

For homeowners researching Garden Annexes London, the project requires more consideration than a standard garden office or leisure room. An annexe may include sleeping accommodation, a bathroom, kitchen facilities, heating, drainage and other services associated with day-to-day living. That means layout, year-round comfort, accessibility, utilities, planning and Building Regulations should all be considered from the beginning.

PECASA takes a design-led approach, developing each building around the property, available garden space, intended occupants and the way the annexe will be used now and in the future.

What Is a Garden Annexe?

A garden annexe is a separate building within the grounds of a main home that provides additional accommodation connected with the needs of the household. 

Depending on the approved design and intended use, an annexe can incorporate: 

  • A bedroom or sleeping area 
  • A private living space 
  • Shower room or bathroom 
  • Kitchen or kitchenette 
  • Built-in storage 
  • Heating and cooling 
  • Internet and data connections 
  • Private entrance 
  • Accessible circulation 
  • Outdoor seating or decking 

Unlike a simple summerhouse, an annexe is normally intended to provide a much higher standard of comfort and functionality. 

It may be occupied regularly and therefore needs proper consideration of insulation, ventilation, heating, electrical capacity, water supply, drainage, glazing, privacy and internal layout. 

For many families, the objective is not to create an unrelated second property. It is to give a relative greater independence while retaining a practical connection with the main household.

Who Can Benefit From a Bespoke Annexe?

There is no single standard annexe brief. The right layout depends on who will use the building and how their requirements may develop. 

Multi-Generational Living 

An annexe can provide parents or grandparents with their own private space while allowing family members to remain close. 

This can create a useful balance between independence and connection. An older relative can have their own bedroom, bathroom and living space without being isolated from the family living in the main house. 

The design can also take future requirements into account rather than simply meeting today’s needs. 

Space for Adult Children 

Where an adult child would benefit from greater independence but moving elsewhere is not practical, an annexe may provide additional personal space within the family property, subject to the necessary permissions. 

A good layout can create separation between living, sleeping and working areas even where the available footprint is relatively compact. 

Accommodation for Relatives and Guests 

Families who frequently accommodate relatives or long-term guests may prefer a dedicated space instead of permanently sacrificing bedrooms within the main house. 

The annexe can provide privacy for visitors while allowing them to remain conveniently close to the household. 

Flexible Family Accommodation 

Household requirements change over time. 

A building created initially for a parent may eventually serve another family member or be adapted for another ancillary purpose, provided the relevant planning conditions and permissions continue to be observed. 

Designing flexibility into the layout can therefore add considerable long-term value. 

Designing Around Everyday Living

The most effective annexe layout starts with how the occupant will actually move through and use the space. 

A compact design may need to combine sleeping and living functions efficiently. A larger annexe may allow a separate bedroom, lounge, bathroom, kitchen area and storage. 

Furniture placement should be considered before doors, glazing, sockets and service locations are finalised. 

Important questions include: 

  • Who will occupy the annexe? 
  • Will it be used occasionally or every day? 
  • Is step-free access important? 
  • Does the occupant need wider circulation areas? 
  • Would a walk-in shower be preferable? 
  • Is a full kitchen necessary or would a kitchenette be sufficient? 
  • How much storage will be required? 
  • How important is direct access to the garden? 
  • Where should windows sit to provide daylight while protecting privacy? 
  • How will the occupant move between the annexe and the main house? 
  • Could the occupant’s mobility needs change in future? 

These decisions are far easier to address during design than after construction.

Designing for Older Parents and Accessible Living

For many households, an annexe is considered because an older parent wants greater independence without living far away from family. 

In that situation, small design decisions become particularly important. 

A suitable layout may consider: 

  • Reduced or flush thresholds 
  • Wider door openings 
  • Comfortable circulation around furniture 
  • Easy-access shower facilities 
  • Non-slip flooring 
  • Convenient socket and switch positions 
  • Good natural and artificial lighting 
  • Accessible storage 
  • Space for future mobility aids 
  • A straightforward route to the main house 

Accessibility does not mean the room needs to look clinical. These features can be integrated discreetly into a contemporary interior. 

Thinking ahead can also reduce the need for expensive alterations later. 

Built for Comfortable Year-Round Use

Residential-style accommodation should be designed as a comfortable building rather than simply an attractive garden structure.  PECASA’s published specification includes Structural Insulated Panels (SIPs), ground-screw foundations, EPDM roofing, high-performance glazing, finished interiors, flooring, lighting and electrical installations. PECASA also offers heating and air-conditioning options. 

SIP construction combines structural strength with insulation within the building envelope. PECASA identifies SIPs as a core component of its standard garden-room and annexe construction system.  For accommodation used regularly, however, the complete building also needs to consider ventilation, moisture management, glazing performance, heating and the services required by the occupant.  The specification should therefore reflect the proposed use rather than simply copying the specification of a standard garden office.

Foundations and Structural Construction

Every successful building starts with the foundations. 

PECASA uses ground-screw foundations within its published build standard. 

On suitable sites, ground screws can provide a foundation system without the extent of excavation associated with some conventional concrete solutions. However, site conditions, levels, loads and the final building design still need to be assessed for each project. 

Above the foundation system, walls, floor, roof, glazing and external finishes work together to create the building envelope. 

The exterior can then be designed to complement the architecture of the main home or create a contrasting contemporary feature within the garden.

Insulation, Heating and Energy Comfort

An annexe intended for regular occupation needs to remain comfortable during both colder and warmer periods. 

Insulation is therefore only one part of the solution. 

The design should consider:

  • Wall insulation 
  • Roof insulation 
  • Floor insulation 
  • Window and door performance 
  • Air leakage 

Wall insulation 

  • Ventilation 
  • Solar gain 
  • Heating requirements 
  • Cooling requirements

Large areas of glazing can provide excellent daylight and garden views but also affect heat loss and summer temperatures. 

The glazing strategy should therefore be considered alongside orientation, shading and the room’s intended use.

Bathrooms, Showers, Water and Drainage

A bathroom or shower room can transform an annexe into a far more practical living space, but it also introduces additional technical requirements. 

The design needs to consider: 

  • Cold-water supply 
  • Hot-water provision 
  • Wastewater drainage 
  • Shower or bath location 
  • WC connection 
  • Waterproof finishes 
  • Mechanical extraction 
  • Electrical safety 
  • Maintenance access 

The location of existing drainage can have a significant impact on the practical design and project cost. 

For this reason, bathroom positioning should be considered early rather than added after the building layout has already been finalised.

Kitchen and Kitchenette Design

Some annexes require only a small kitchenette for drinks, breakfast and light food preparation. Others may require more extensive kitchen facilities. 

The correct solution depends on the intended use and any planning restrictions applying to the accommodation. 

A kitchen design may need space for: 

  • Sink and water supply 
  • Refrigerator 
  • Storage units 
  • Worktops 
  • Microwave or oven 
  • Hob 
  • Extraction 
  • Dishwasher 
  • Dedicated electrical supplies 

Kitchen requirements also interact with drainage, ventilation and the overall floor plan. 

PECASA can therefore design these facilities as part of the wider building rather than treating them as an afterthought. 

Electrical Installation and Connectivity

Modern living requires more than a small number of power sockets. 

Depending on the occupant, the annexe may require: 

  • General power sockets 
  • USB charging 
  • Dedicated kitchen circuits 
  • Bathroom electrical provisions 
  • Internal and external lighting 
  • Heating or air-conditioning supply 
  • Television connection 
  • Wi-Fi or wired data connection 
  • Security lighting 
  • Smart controls 
  • Exterior sockets 

If the occupant works remotely, regularly makes video calls or uses streaming services, internet connectivity should be addressed during design. 

Planning Permission for an Annexe

Planning is one of the most important differences between an annexe and a standard garden office or hobby room. 

Government technical guidance explains that permitted development rights for ordinary household outbuildings do not cover normal residential uses such as separate self-contained accommodation or an outbuilding being used for primary living accommodation including bedrooms, bathrooms or kitchens. 

Planning Portal similarly explains that the standard outbuilding rules do not cover separate self-contained living accommodation. 

This means a residential annexe should not automatically be assumed to have the same permitted-development status as a shed, gym, studio or garden office. 

The correct planning route depends on factors including: 

  • The proposed use 
  • Size and scale 
  • Relationship with the main dwelling 
  • Level of independence 
  • Site layout 
  • Existing planning conditions 
  • Listed-building status 
  • Conservation-area restrictions 
  • Article 4 directions 
  • Local planning policy 

The relevant local planning authority ultimately determines the planning position for an individual property.

Building Regulations for Annexes

Planning permission and Building Regulations are separate matters. 

Planning Portal explains that certain small detached outbuildings can sometimes fall outside the requirement for Building Regulations approval, but the commonly referenced exemptions apply where the building contains no sleeping accommodation. 

A residential annexe containing a bedroom should therefore not simply be treated in the same way as a basic garden shed or summerhouse. 

Depending on the design and use, Building Regulations considerations can include: 

  • Structural safety 
  • Fire safety 
  • Thermal performance 
  • Ventilation 
  • Drainage 
  • Electrical installation 
  • Sanitation 
  • Glazing 
  • Energy efficiency 

These requirements should be established early so that they can be incorporated into the design rather than addressed retrospectively. 

Garden Annexes London – Designed for Real London Properties

London properties present a broad range of site conditions. 

A detached suburban home with wide side access will have very different requirements from a terraced property with restricted rear-garden access. 

Important considerations may include: 

  • Narrow access routes 
  • Neighbouring boundaries 
  • Existing extensions 
  • Mature trees 
  • Changes in garden level 
  • Existing patios or landscaping 
  • Drainage routes 
  • Underground utilities 
  • Privacy 
  • Overlooking 
  • Delivery access 

PECASA’s bespoke design process means the footprint, glazing, services and internal layout can respond to the individual property rather than forcing the homeowner into a predetermined catalogue design. 

Privacy is particularly important on urban sites. Window positioning, screening, landscaping and external materials can all help the annexe integrate more successfully into its surroundings. 

Council Tax Considerations

Homeowners should also understand that creating residential-style accommodation can have Council Tax implications. 

Current GOV.UK guidance states that annexes are generally subject to a separate Council Tax bill, although discounts or exemptions can apply in qualifying circumstances. 

For example, certain annexes occupied by relatives or used as part of the main home can qualify for reductions. Specific exemptions may also apply in some circumstances. 

Whether an annexe receives a separate assessment depends on its characteristics and use. 

 

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Garden Annexe need planning permission?

It may. Standard outbuilding permitted-development rights do not extend to separate, self-contained living accommodation. The exact position depends on the proposed use, design, property and local restrictions, so the project should be checked with the relevant local planning authority before construction.

Does an annexe need Building Regulations approval?

Potentially, yes. Buildings containing sleeping accommodation should not be assumed to qualify for exemptions that can apply to some small detached outbuildings. Building Regulations should be assessed against the proposed design and intended use.

Can an annexe have a kitchen and bathroom?

Potentially, yes. These facilities affect plumbing, drainage, ventilation and electrics and may also influence the planning assessment. Kitchen and bathroom requirements should therefore be incorporated from the beginning of the design process.

Can an annexe be designed for an elderly parent?

Yes, subject to the required planning and regulatory approvals. The layout can prioritise privacy, accessible circulation, practical bathroom arrangements, natural light and convenient proximity to the main house.

Can an annexe be used all year round?

A properly specified annexe can be designed for year-round occupation, subject to the necessary permissions and Building Regulations requirements. Insulation, glazing, heating or cooling, ventilation and appropriate services should all be considered according to how the building will be used.

How much does a bespoke annexe cost?

Cost depends on size, layout, glazing, finishes, kitchens or bathrooms, utilities, site access and professional or planning requirements. A site-specific design and detailed specification provide a more useful figure than relying on a generic price per square metre.
